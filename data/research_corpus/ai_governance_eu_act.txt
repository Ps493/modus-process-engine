The EU AI Act, in force since August 2024, establishes a four-tier risk
classification for AI systems — unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal
risk — with compliance obligations scaling directly with assessed risk.
Minimal-risk systems face no mandatory requirements, while systems
classified as high-risk (per Annex III, covering areas such as employment,
biometrics, critical infrastructure, essential services, and
administration of justice) must complete a formal conformity assessment,
produce extensive technical documentation, register in an EU database,
implement a risk management system, establish data governance measures,
build in human oversight mechanisms, and demonstrate accuracy, robustness,
and incident-logging capability before market entry. The phased
implementation timeline has shifted: prohibitions on unacceptable-risk
practices became enforceable in February 2025, general-purpose AI model
provider obligations applied from August 2025, and — following a Digital
Omnibus amendment enacted in mid-2026 — the full high-risk system
obligations were postponed, now applying from December 2027 (Annex III
systems) and August 2028 (Annex I systems) rather than the originally
planned August 2026 date. For enterprise AI governance purposes, the
practical takeaway repeated across compliance guidance is that
organisations should not wait for enforcement deadlines: AI inventories,
risk classification, model documentation, and defined human-oversight/
escalation paths are increasingly expected by customers, boards, and
regulators regardless of the exact enforcement date, and are far cheaper
to build in from the start than to retrofit under deadline pressure.
