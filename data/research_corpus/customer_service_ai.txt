Conversational AI adoption in customer service has moved from experimental
to mainstream: industry surveys report that the large majority of contact
centres have implemented some form of AI-powered support, and analyst
forecasts (Gartner-cited) project agentic AI systems will be able to
autonomously resolve a large share of common, routine service requests
within the next few years, interpreting intent, retrieving information, and
completing multi-step actions rather than just answering FAQs. However,
adoption headlines significantly outrun customer sentiment: independent
survey data (SurveyMonkey, December 2025, ~2,000 US respondents) found a
strong majority of consumers still prefer speaking with a human agent over
an AI agent, with trust cited as the central issue — most respondents
believe human agents provide more accurate support, and confidence in
being able to tell whether they're talking to a bot or a person is mixed.
This gap matters operationally: routine, high-volume, low-ambiguity
interactions (order status, simple FAQs, basic complaint triage) are the
strongest fit for automation, while complex complaints, escalations, and
interactions involving frustrated or high-value customers continue to
require human agents, with AI increasingly used to summarise context and
suggest responses to the human rather than to resolve the interaction
autonomously. Younger customers show somewhat higher openness to AI-first
service than older customers, but the preference for human contact
persists across all age groups surveyed.
