Retailers using AI-driven demand forecasting are seeing meaningfully better
accuracy than traditional statistical methods. Industry reporting that cites
McKinsey research consistently points to forecast error reductions in the
20-50% range when machine learning models replace manual or purely
statistical forecasting, with knock-on effects including fewer stockouts,
leaner overall inventory levels (McKinsey estimates 20-30% reductions), and
higher order fill rates. These gains come from models that continuously
retrain on real sales signals rather than static historical averages, so
they adapt faster to changing demand patterns, seasonality, and the effect
of promotions. The same body of research notes that AI-driven forecasting
and inventory optimisation is one of the most mature and highest-adoption
AI use cases in retail specifically because the underlying data (point-of-
sale transactions, inventory levels, promotional calendars) is already
well-structured, which lowers the barrier to building reliable models
compared with less structured domains like customer service or creative
merchandising decisions. Reported cost impacts include reduced logistics
and procurement costs alongside reduced markdown/waste from overstocking.
