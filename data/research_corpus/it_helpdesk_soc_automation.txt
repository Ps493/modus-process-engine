IT operations and cybersecurity functions show some of the clearest
evidence of AI shifting from full manual handling toward automated triage
with human oversight for exceptions and high-stakes decisions. In security
operations centres specifically, industry surveys (Gurucul's 2025 Pulse of
the AI SOC report, cited across multiple analyses) report that a large
majority of organisations have successfully automated routine alert
triage, with AI-augmented SOCs detecting threats roughly 50% faster and
reducing analyst triage workload by around 60% in aggregate industry
figures. Critically, this is described consistently across sources as
augmentation rather than replacement: security leaders expect AI to
handle a majority of routine SOC workload within a few years, but human
analysts remain responsible for complex investigation, threat hunting, and
strategic response decisions — the automation targets the repetitive
first-pass triage step specifically, not the judgement-heavy investigation
that follows. IT helpdesk ticket automation shows a similar pattern: a
2025 Forrester study cited in industry writeups found enterprises
deploying AI-augmented IT support saw average three-year ROI around 215%,
with payback periods averaging just over a year, concentrated in
mid-to-large organisations with high ticket volume where the fixed cost of
implementation is spread across more resolved tickets. Patch management
is highlighted as one of the most automated and fastest-growing IT
security functions — industry surveys report roughly 70% of companies
have automated basic patch management operations, with automated patch
deployment continuing to grow — reflecting that patching is highly
rule-based (apply verified vendor updates on a schedule) and therefore a
strong automation candidate with low judgement requirements, similar in
profile to accounts payable in finance.
